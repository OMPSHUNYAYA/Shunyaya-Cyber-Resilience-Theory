# SCRT Research Summary v2.7.0

Shunyaya Cyber Resilience Theory develops exact continuation semantics for cyber-defense architectures whose operational capability and independent assurance capability can evolve differently under compromise, hardening, recovery, and adversarial action.

The central cyber distinction is

`operational survival != certified survival`.

SCRT represents operational routes, evidence-bearing certified routes, shared dependency ancestry, attacker impacts, recovery responses, and future structural continuation. It derives a strict hierarchy of canonical states because stronger future operation/observer contracts can expose information that weaker contracts cannot observe.

The integrated theorem uses a continuation contract

`C=(S,G,O)`

and defines contextual equivalence by equality of `O` after every admitted finite continuation over `G`. The quotient is continuation-complete and information-minimal up to injective recoding of semantic classes.

Three semantic levels are separated explicitly:

`EXACT_STRUCTURAL`

`TARGET_RELATIVE`

`CAMPAIGN_RESIDUAL`.

Exact structural semantics is finite-dimensional but may have infinitely many contextual states because exact multiplicity remains observable. For fixed resilience target `k`, sharp saturation at `k` gives a finite exact quotient for target-congruent continuation. For finite registered campaign semantics, residual minimization gives the coarsest right congruence preserving every future campaign-value profile.

The cyber-resilience specialization develops role-polarized silent-assurance obstructions, coupled attacker impacts, dual recovery-response antichains, one-shot assurance contest kernels, round-resolved campaign values, and pure campaign residual equivalence.

The package includes written theorem statements and proofs, an explicit modeling/classification encoding contract, exact reference algorithms, principal and independent verification paths, a cross-version regression chain, worked examples, machine-readable theorem records, and cryptographic binding of the frozen computational core.
