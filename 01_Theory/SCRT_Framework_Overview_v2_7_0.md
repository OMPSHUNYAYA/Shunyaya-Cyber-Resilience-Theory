# SCRT Framework Overview v2.7.0

## 1. Objective

SCRT develops exact mathematical semantics for cyber resilience under shared ancestry, operational defense, independent assurance evidence, compromise, hardening, adaptive recovery, and future attacker/defender continuation.

The central distinction is:

`operational capacity` versus `certified capacity`.

A cyber architecture may remain operational while losing the independent evidence structure required to certify that operation.

## 2. Primitive objects

A finite SCRT model declares:

- named security obligations;
- operational defensive routes;
- typed certified signatures `(D,E)`;
- finite dependency ancestry;
- compromise channels;
- recovery and hardening actions;
- resource and cost declarations;
- an admitted continuation grammar;
- a declared observer contract.

`D` denotes defensive/control ancestry and `E` denotes evidentiary/assurance ancestry.

## 3. Kernel hierarchy

The hierarchy is generated by stronger future semantics.

`BRK` retains passive minimal operational supports and minimal typed certified signatures.

`IRK` retains exact latent realization multiplicities when structural diversification can expose duplicates.

`ORIK` retains multiplicity by named obligation when targeted intervention can expose obligation ownership.

Transformation kernels retain structural hardening operators, costs, and resources when future hardening can modify the architecture.

Gauge quotients remove ancestry labels that carry no semantic authority while preserving anchored identities.

Target-relative kernels clip multiplicities at `k` when only target-`k` questions are observed.

Campaign kernels retain round granularity and joint ancestry correlation when sequential attacks and recoveries can expose timing structure.

`CRK` minimizes the finite campaign state by exact future campaign values.

## 4. Integrated principle

A canonical state is not operation-independent.

For a continuation contract `C=(S,G,O)`, the minimum sufficient state is the quotient of `S` by equality under every finite continuation generated by `G` and observed through `O`.

Strengthening `G` or `O` may require a strict refinement. Weakening them may permit a strict quotient.
