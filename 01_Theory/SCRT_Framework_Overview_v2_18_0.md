# SCRT Framework Overview v2.18.0

## Objective

Shunyaya Cyber Resilience Theory (SCRT) develops exact mathematical semantics for finite declared cyber-resilience architectures under shared dependency ancestry, compromise, hardening, recovery, evidence loss, adversarial action, and future continuation.

Its central cyber distinction is:

`operational capacity C_op` versus `independently certified capacity C_cert`.

A system may remain operational at target while the declared independent basis for trusting that operation falls below target. SCRT calls this condition **silent assurance failure**:

`C_op >= k and C_cert < k`.

## Two principal theorem pillars

### Continuation and campaign classification

For a declared continuation contract `C=(S,G,O)`, SCRT classifies states by equality of all admitted future observations. Stronger future-operation or observer contracts can require strictly finer canonical states. Exact structural, target-relative, and campaign-residual semantics are kept distinct.

### Assurance-coherent hardening phase theory

Operational-only hardening can enlarge the silent-assurance region. SCRT characterizes the exact newly exposed scenarios and the minimum certified compensation needed to make the hardening assurance-coherent.

At target `k`, operational activation witnesses and individual certified responses have local rank at most `k`. The global behavior then splits:

`conflict-free certified resources -> global audit order k -> fixed-k explicit decision in P`

`exclusive certified resources -> no global audit order bounded by k -> NP-complete already at k=1`.

The hard phase occurs even when local activation and response witnesses are singletons.

## Modeling boundary

SCRT classifies admitted mathematical encodings. It does not infer that a deployed system has been encoded completely, that evidence is authentic, that controls are correctly implemented, or that the registered attacker/action grammar exhausts reality.

## Mathematical positioning

SCRT uses established mathematical structures where appropriate, including finite-state behavioral equivalence, right congruences, partition refinement, saturation, packing/blocker duality, fixed-point reasoning, matching/resource compatibility, finite optimization, and complexity reductions. These general mechanisms are not claimed as SCRT inventions. SCRT's mathematical contribution is the independently derived cyber-resilience semantics and the integrated theorem structure proved under its declared contracts.
